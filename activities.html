Activities & Experiences

From swimming with whale sharks to cooking Bicol Express from scratch, the region's experiences split cleanly across four chapters — adventure, culture, food, and nature. Pick what suits your pace.

Category 01 of 04

Adventure

Chase the active side of Bicol.

Volcano trails, cable wakeparks, sea-cave kayaking, and a chance to share the water with the world's largest fish.

Five ways to break a sweat

Most adventure operators are clustered around Legazpi and Naga and can be booked the day before. Always carry water and start early — midday heat on Mayon's lava trails can be punishing.

  • ATV ride on Mayon lava trails

    Guided all-terrain-vehicle circuits across the 2006 lava flow deposits — black sand fields with a clear view of the crater. Sessions run 1 to 3 hours from the Ligñon Hill staging area.

    Ligñon Hill · Legazpi · PHP 1,500+
  • Whale shark interaction (butanding)

    Snorkel-only, WWF-regulated encounters with whale sharks off Donsol. Boats leave at 7 am; each session allows a maximum of six swimmers per shark, no touching, no flash photography.

    Donsol · Sorsogon · Feb – May
  • Cable wakeboarding at CWC

    Southeast Asia's first full-cable wakepark. Beginners get a free 15-minute orientation; full-day passes include helmet, life vest, and unlimited rides around the lagoon.

    Pili · Camarines Sur · PHP 350/hr
  • Trek Mount Isarog

    A two-day hike through mossy forest to the summit of an extinct stratovolcano — home to the world's smallest flower (Rafflesia) when in bloom. Permits required at the PENRO office in Naga.

    Naga · Camarines Sur · 2 days
  • Spelunking at Hoydoy-Hoydoy Cave

    A 2-hour guided crawl through Hoydoy-Hoydoy's limestone chambers near Camaligan — flowstones, bats, and an underground river passage that empties into a jungle pool.

    Camaligan · Camarines Sur · PHP 800

Category 02 of 04

Culture

Walk into Bicol's festivals.

The region's festival calendar is anchored on the Peñafrancia in September — the country's largest Marian devotion — with colourful folkloric and harvest festivals running from May to October.

Four festivals worth the trip

Bicol's festivals mix pre-Hispanic epic, Catholic devotion, and street-party spectacle. Time your visit to coincide with at least one — the streets fill completely, so book hotels 6+ weeks ahead.

  • Peñafrancia Festival (Ina)

    The country's largest Marian procession — 9 days of novena, fluvial parade on the Bicol River, and a million devotees walking behind Ina, the image of Our Lady of Peñafrancia.

    Naga · September
  • Ibalong Festival

    A street-theatre epic based on the Bicolano myth of the three heroes — Baltog, Handiong, and Bantong — who slew the region's monsters. Participants wear giant masks and hand-painted capes.

    Legazpi · October
  • Magayon Festival

    A month-long celebration of all things Albay — Mayon hikes, street food fairs, a cooking contest for the best Bicol Express, and the Mutya ng Magayon beauty pageant.

    Albay · May
  • Kaogma Festival

    Camarines Sur's founding-anniversary party — five days of concerts, street dance competitions, and food markets on the capitol grounds in Pili, ending in a grand fireworks show.

    Pili · May

Category 03 of 04

Food

The spiciest kitchen in the country.

Coconut milk and long green chilies are the backbone of every Bicolano meal. Bring an appetite — and a tolerance for heat.

Tastes you have to try

Bicolano cuisine uses coconut milk (gata) and finger chilies (siling mahaba) almost everywhere. Don't leave without trying the four dishes below — and bring home pili-nut brittle from the airport.

  • Bicol Express

    Pork belly simmered in coconut milk, shrimp paste, and finger-length chilies — named after the Manila–Bicol train that brought the recipe to the capital. Invented in the 1970s by a Naga-born cook.

    Region-wide · medium-heat
  • Laing

    Dried taro leaves slow-cooked in coconut milk with ginger, garlic, and chilies until creamy and smoky. Served as a side or as a main with rice; the leaves must be fully cooked to neutralise their calcium oxalate.

    Region-wide · high-heat
  • Pinangat

    Taro leaves wrapped around minced pork or fish, tied with coconut midrib, and simmered in coconut milk. A specialty of Camaligan — try it at the roadside eateries along the highway to Naga.

    Camaligan · medium-heat
  • Pili nut sweets

    The pili tree grows wild across Bicol, and its buttery nut — roasted, caramelised, or made into brittle — is the region's most exported snack. Pick up tarts, marzipan, and unsalted roasted pili at any public market.

    Albay · sweet · souvenir-friendly

Category 04 of 04

Nature

When the pace needs to slow.

Quiet beaches, mangrove rivers, fireflies, and hot springs — the softer side of the region, ideal for the day after a hike.

Slow ways to spend a day

Most nature experiences can be booked same-day through your hotel's front desk. Firefly tours run only on moonless nights.

  • Caramoan island hopping

    Full-day banca tours hit 4–5 islands: Matukad, Lahos, Pitogo, and Sabitang Largo. Bring a packed lunch — only a couple of islands have eateries.

    Sabang port · PHP 2,000/boat
  • Firefly watching, Donsol River

    Evening banca rides up the Donsol mangrove corridor — thousands of synchronised fireflies light up the trees like Christmas. Book on a moonless night for the strongest effect.

    Donsol · 6 pm – 8 pm · PHP 550
  • Panicuason hot springs

    Four natural thermal pools at the foot of Mount Isarog, piped in from the volcano's geothermal vents. The site also has a hanging bridge, hike trails, and cold-water pools for contrast.

    Naga · Camarines Sur · PHP 200
  • Misibis Bay beach day

    Day passes to Misibis include a private white-sand cove, an infinity pool over the Pacific, and access to the cliffside zip-line. Boat from Legazpi takes 20 minutes.

    Bacacay · Albay · PHP 3,500/day
