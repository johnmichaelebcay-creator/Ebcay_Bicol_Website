01 — Introduction

A region of contrasts

One volcano, two seas, and a kitchen that bites back.

The Bicol Region occupies the southeastern tail of Luzon, a long peninsula that reaches toward the Visayas and faces the open Pacific. It is defined by a single geological protagonist — Mount Mayon, the world's most perfectly conical volcano — and by the warm waters that lap its coastline, where whale sharks gather each spring to feed on plankton blooms off Donsol.

Bicolanos speak Bikol, cook with coconut milk and finger chilies, and stage some of the largest religious festivals in the country. Spanish friars arrived in 1569 and built stone churches that still stand today, though many were buried by Mayon's eruptions — most famously the Cagsawa church, whose bell tower is all that remains above ground.

The region is generous with its contrasts: black-sand beaches at the foot of a fiery mountain, hidden lagoons behind limestone cliffs, abaca weavers whose craft predates colonization, and a deep Catholic devotion anchored on the Peñafrancia, the largest Marian festival in the Philippines.

Featured destination

The hidden islands of Caramoan.

Cam Sur · Camarines Sur

Island hopping · Limestones · Lagoons

The Philippines' secret archipelago.

Long closed to mass tourism and reachable only by boat from Sabang, Caramoan is a scatter of limestone islands wrapped in white sand and hidden lagoons. The same shores that hosted multiple seasons of the reality show Survivor remain strikingly empty most weeks of the year. Kayak through sea caves, swim in lagoons open only at low tide, and end the day on a sandbar that disappears with the tide.

Culture & character

Three things Bicolanos are known for.

Festivals

Devotion on the river

For 313 years, Naga has closed the streets every September to carry Ina — the Peñafrancia — down the Bicol River on a flotilla of bancas. It is the largest Marian gathering in the country.

Cuisine

Coconut & fire

Bicolano cooking is built on gata (coconut milk) and siling mahaba (long chilies). Bicol Express, laing, and pinangat each arrive at the table steaming, creamy, and unmistakably spicy.

Crafts

Abaca & pili

Bicol grows most of the country's abaca — the world's strongest natural fiber. Weavers turn it into rope, textile, and intricately dyed mats; pili trees supply the country's most prized nut.
